\section{Results\label{sec:results}}

The numbers below come from a full rerun of the pipeline described in Section~\ref{sec:method} against the AIDev Zenodo v3 release (DOI \texttt{10.5281/zenodo.16919272}), using the final 12-indicator activity profile, $\log(1+x)$ preprocessing, and a corrected RQ2 data source. They replace figures from two earlier, less reliable versions of the analysis: one with several data-source bugs, and a second, bug-fixed version whose clustering preprocessing (quantile transformation) was later found unstable (Section~\ref{sec:threats}) and replaced with $\log(1+x)$.

\subsection{RQ1: How does a developer's experience correlate with the frequency of using AI agents?}

For each of the three activity profiles, Table~\ref{tab:rq1-summary} reports the number of developers, and the mean, standard deviation, and median number of Agentic-PRs. The Kruskal--Wallis test showed a statistically significant difference in Agentic-PR counts across the three profiles ($H = 22762.32$, $p < 10^{-300}$, $\varepsilon^2 = 0.32$, bootstrap 95\% CI $[0.314, 0.323]$) -- a large effect by conventional standards, unlike the other two research questions.

All three pairwise Mann--Whitney comparisons (two-sided, Bonferroni-adjusted $\alpha = 0.0167$) were significant:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Senior vs.\ Junior}: $U = 3.665\times10^{8}$, $p < 10^{-253}$, rank-biserial $r = -0.175$, 95\% CI $[-0.186, -0.165]$.
    \item \textbf{Senior vs.\ Mid}: $U = 2.626\times10^{7}$, $p < 10^{-300}$, rank-biserial $r = 0.703$, 95\% CI $[0.694, 0.711]$.
    \item \textbf{Mid vs.\ Junior}: $U = 5.199\times10^{8}$, $p < 10^{-300}$, rank-biserial $r = -0.825$, 95\% CI $[-0.829, -0.820]$.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{The result is not monotonic in activity level.} The \emph{Mid} profile has by far the highest Agentic-PR volume (mean 42.1, median 12), well above both \emph{Senior} (mean 7.9, median 2) and \emph{Junior} (mean 3.6, median 1); Senior in turn has a modestly but significantly higher volume than Junior. Recall that the profiles are ranked by a composite of all 12 indicators, not by Agentic-PR count itself (Section~\ref{sec:method}): Senior outranks Mid on that composite because of indicators such as followers, account tenure, and repository stars/forks, even though Mid submits far more Agentic-PRs. Two of the three pairwise effect sizes are large (Senior-vs-Mid $r=0.70$, Mid-vs-Junior $r=-0.82$), consistent with the large omnibus effect size; only the Senior-vs-Junior contrast is modest ($r=-0.18$).

\subsection{RQ2: Does human experience translate into a higher acceptance rate of agent-generated code?}

Table~\ref{tab:rq2-summary} reports the per-developer proportion of resolved Agentic-PRs that were merged. The Kruskal--Wallis test confirmed a significant difference across profiles ($H = 2380.03$, $p < 10^{-300}$, $\varepsilon^2 = 0.043$, bootstrap 95\% CI $[0.039, 0.046]$). All three pairwise comparisons were significant:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Senior vs.\ Junior}: $U = 1.583\times10^{8}$, $p < 10^{-259}$, rank-biserial $r = 0.151$, 95\% CI $[0.142, 0.160]$.
    \item \textbf{Senior vs.\ Mid}: $U = 7.458\times10^{7}$, $p < 10^{-3}$, rank-biserial $r = -0.022$, 95\% CI $[-0.035, -0.009]$.
    \item \textbf{Mid vs.\ Junior}: $U = 1.537\times10^{8}$, $p < 10^{-300}$, rank-biserial $r = 0.210$, 95\% CI $[0.200, 0.219]$.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Mid has the highest mean acceptance rate (91.8\%), narrowly ahead of Junior (90.2\%), with Senior clearly lowest (83.2\%).} The Senior-vs-Mid effect is statistically significant but negligible in size ($r=-0.02$); the more substantial contrasts are Senior's lower acceptance against both other profiles. This is not the strict monotonic decrease reported in an earlier version of this analysis under quantile-transform-based clustering, but the qualitative conclusion that motivated that revision is unchanged: \emph{higher composite GitHub activity is not associated with a higher agent-PR acceptance rate} -- the highest-activity profile (Senior) has the lowest acceptance in both versions of the analysis.

\subsection{RQ3: How does the acceptance rate of Agentic-PRs compare to a human-authored baseline?}

As described in Section~\ref{sec:method}, this is a population-level comparison, not stratified by activity profile, so it is unaffected by the change in clustering preprocessing: the full Agentic-PR population (from RQ2, pooled across profiles) against the independent \texttt{human\_pull\_request} baseline. Table~\ref{tab:rq3-summary} reports the descriptive statistics for both groups.

A two-sided Mann--Whitney $U$ test found a statistically significant difference ($U = 6.795\times10^{7}$, $p < 10^{-8}$, rank-biserial $r = -0.054$, bootstrap 95\% CI $[-0.075, -0.034]$). \textbf{Agentic-PRs are accepted at a modestly higher rate (88.9\%) than the human-authored baseline (78.6\%)}, though the effect size is small and the human-authored baseline has a much wider spread (SD 38.8 vs.\ 26.8). This comparison is not developer-matched: the two populations overlap in only 351 of 2,515 human-baseline authors, so the difference reflects two different populations of contributors to two different (though both high-activity) parts of GitHub, not a within-person contrast of "the same developer with vs.\ without an agent."
