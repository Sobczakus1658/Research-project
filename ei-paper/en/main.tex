\begin{abstract}[en]
\textbf{Context}:
Nowadays, growing and increasingly frequent use of AI agents can be seen in public repositories. However, the way they are adopted and the results obtained, depending on the developer's experience level, remain unclear.
\\
\textbf{Objective}:
We examined the relationship between developer experience and how often AI assistance is used, as well as the acceptance of pull requests linked to agents, also comparing the acceptance of agent-based contributions with an independent, baseline human population.
\\
\textbf{Method}:
In this paper we analyzed the AIDev dataset from the MSR 2026 Mining Challenge: 932,791 pull requests created by 72,189 users in more than 116,000 public repositories up to August 2025. The data were enriched with GitHub GraphQL activity metrics. After preprocessing (log(1+x) plus standardization) and removing 1\% of outliers, 71,467 developers remained. For clustering, the k-means algorithm was used with k=3 on the standardized 12-dimensional statistic, splitting developers into activity profiles named Junior, Mid, and Senior. In addition, PCA was used to visualize the most dominant features. In the preprocessing step, a quantile transformation was tried first, but this method turned out to be unstable in the complete-case sensitivity check (the Adjusted Rand Index was 0.46, compared to as much as 0.98 for log(1+x)). Differences in the number of Agentic-PRs and in the acceptance rate between groups were tested with the Kruskal-Wallis test and Mann-Whitney tests with a Bonferroni correction.
\\
\textbf{Results}:
Agent-use intensity was significantly non-monotonic ($\varepsilon^2=0.32$). The intermediate-activity cluster, named Mid, showed the highest agent-use activity (median 12), higher than Junior (median 1) or the highest-activity cluster (median 2). The acceptance rate was highest for Mid (91.8\%) and lowest for Senior (83.2\%), with Junior in between (90.2\%); all three pairwise comparisons were statistically significant, though with a much smaller effect size ($\varepsilon^2=0.043$) than for agent-use intensity. Agentic-PRs were accepted at a slightly, but statistically significantly, higher rate (88.9\%) than the independent baseline human population (78.6\%).
\\
\textbf{Conclusions}:
Agent use and contribution outcomes did not follow a simple experience gradient: the highest-activity cluster had neither the most Agentic-PRs nor the highest acceptance rate. Activity-based clusters should not be equated with verified professional experience.
\end{abstract}
